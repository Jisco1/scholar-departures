How to use this board

1 · Find routes you qualify for

Filter by region, level and field. A university route means the school itself waives tuition or funds you; a programme is an award you win first and take to a school that admits you.

2 · Read the route page

Every card links to its own page: what the money covers, who can apply, the steps, and the traps people miss — each checked against the official source and dated.

3 · Plan backwards from the deadline

Save routes to your shortlist, compare them side by side, and export deadlines or a week-by-week plan to your calendar. Submit early — portals slow down on the last day.

Deadlines shown are each programme's typical annual window — always confirm on the official page, since cycles shift slightly every year. Data last checked {{LAST_CHECK}}; read how we verify.

Deadline status is computed live against today's date: Approaching means the next typical deadline falls within 120 days; Closed means this cycle's window has passed (or hasn't opened yet) and shows when it next comes around. A ≈ marks programmes whose exact date varies by course or country; Rolling means positions open year-round with no fixed close date.

Your bookmarked shortlist is stored only in this browser, so it's still here when you come back — nothing is sent to us.

All fields on a card means the route accepts any discipline — the fields listed are simply where that school or programme is strongest. Specific fields means eligibility genuinely narrows to those areas; when you filter by field, those specialists are listed first.

Deliberately left out as tuition-free: Norway's public universities (non-EU/EEA tuition since 2023 — though from 2026–27 each Norwegian university may set its own fee policy, so check yours), TU Munich (non-EU fees since winter 2024) Baden-Württemberg's state universities (€1,500 per semester for non-EU students) and, from autumn 2027, bachelor's and master's study in Iceland for non-EEA students — the most common "free tuition in Europe" myths still circulating.
