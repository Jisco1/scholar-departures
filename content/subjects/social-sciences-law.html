If you want to work in government, law, international organisations or civil society, the most generous scholarships in the world were designed with you in mind. Chevening, the Commonwealth scholarships and Fulbright exist to train future leaders, and they fund master's degrees in policy, law, economics, development and international relations more than any other kind.

That shapes how you apply. These schemes ask about leadership, work experience and what you will do when you return home at least as much as about grades. Chevening wants two years of work experience after your first degree, the Commonwealth scholarships ask for a development impact statement, and several schemes require you to go home for a set period afterwards. Write your plans down before you write your essays; our motivation letter guide shows how.

For law in particular, these scholarships fund master's degrees (LLMs). Fewer schemes fund a first degree in law; Türkiye Bursları and Stipendium Hungaricum are among those that cover bachelor's study.

Doctoral candidates in the social sciences have a second path: salaried PhD posts in Scandinavia, advertised like jobs, often within research projects on conflict, education and psychology. See our funded positions.
