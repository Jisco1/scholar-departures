Engineering is the subject where funding is easiest to find. Three things work in your favour. Germany's public universities charge no tuition, whatever your nationality. Austria's charge far less than most of Europe. And across Scandinavia, Switzerland and the Netherlands, a doctorate in engineering is usually a paid job rather than a scholarship you have to win.

For a master's, first decide whether you need your living costs covered. A tuition-free place at RWTH Aachen or a low-fee place at TU Wien still leaves rent and food to pay, and a German student visa asks you to show you can afford them (our Germany guide has the current amount). If you need the whole cost covered, aim for the excellence awards at ETH Zürich, TU Delft and Politecnico di Milano. Each is decided alongside your admission, so the master's application deadline is also the scholarship deadline.

For a PhD, look at advertised positions as well as scholarships. Engineering departments recruit doctoral candidates the way companies recruit staff: a salary, a contract and a defined project. Our list of funded PhD positions carries current openings, and the guide to funded PhDs in Europe explains how salaried posts differ from scholarships.

Engineering admissions are judged on your transcript and on how closely your bachelor's matches the programme. Expect to send module descriptions with your application, and check each programme's English requirement early, because a missing test score is the most common reason a strong engineer misses a deadline.
