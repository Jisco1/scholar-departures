Students of history, languages, philosophy, literature and the arts often hear that there is no money for their subject. That is not quite true. There are fewer subject-specific awards than in the sciences, but the funding that exists is open, and some of it is among the best value anywhere.

The cheapest route is tuition-free study in Central Europe. Freie Universität Berlin is strongest in the humanities and charges no tuition, the University of Vienna charges non-EU students a low fee, and Charles University is free in Czech. The catch is language: many bachelor's degrees are taught in German or Czech, so a year of language study is often part of the plan.

For undergraduates, American liberal arts colleges are the other big opportunity. Amherst and Bowdoin admit international students need-blind and meet their full need, and small classes in the humanities are their speciality.

For postgraduate study, the awards that accept every subject are your best friends: Chevening, Gates Cambridge, Rhodes and Erasmus Mundus all fund humanities degrees. Architects and designers should also look at Politecnico di Milano's merit awards, which can waive the full fee.
